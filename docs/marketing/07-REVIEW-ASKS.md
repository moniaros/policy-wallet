# Deliverable 7 — The two review asks

**Both leave the building today; they carry the longest lead times in the launch plan.** These are
the *asks*, drafted so each reviewer has everything needed to answer — they are not the opinions
and not draft Terms language, per instruction.

## Ask 1 — to insurance-regulation counsel · intermediary status under IDD / ν. 4583/2018

**The question, precisely:**

> Do any of PolicyWallet's activities, as described below, constitute insurance distribution
> (διανομή ασφαλιστικών προϊόντων) within the meaning of Directive (EU) 2016/97 (IDD) as
> transposed by ν. 4583/2018, such that registration as an insurance intermediary is required —
> **(a)** in the current direct-to-consumer deployment, and **(b)** in a contemplated embedded
> deployment, where the same analysis runs inside a bank's or insurer's own app or adviser tools
> under a commercial agreement with that institution?

**What the product does** *(complete list — counsel should assume nothing beyond it)*:
- Reads insurance policy documents the customer uploads (any insurer, any line) and explains, in
  plain language: what is covered, what is excluded, when cover ends, limits, deductibles, waiting
  periods.
- Detects coverage gaps and duplicate cover across the customer's own policies, presented as
  findings to review — the stated next step is always «ερωτήσεις για τον ασφαλιστή ή τον σύμβουλό
  σας», never a product.
- Answers the customer's questions from their own policy text.
- Sends renewal reminders; organises documents; exports and deletes on request.
- Lets the customer share selected policies with their own agent, revocably.

**What the product does not do — design invariants the positioning rests on, not current gaps:**
- Sells no policy, quotes no premium, compares no prices, ranks no insurers or products.
- Recommends nothing: no «αγοράστε», «αλλάξτε», «ακυρώστε» anywhere in the output; copy verbs are
  understand / see / organise / prepare.
- Takes no commission from any insurer; is paid by consumer subscription (and, in the embedded
  case, by the institution for the deployment — **not** per product outcome).
- Labels every output as not regulated insurance advice and not a replacement for the user's agent.
- In the embedded case: never pricing, never underwriting; needs analysis and explanation only.

**Why (b) matters now:** a partner page for institutions is drafted and blocked on this answer. Its
copy would state that embedding does not make the product an intermediary — **that sentence ships
only against your opinion**, and an institution's compliance review will ask for the basis.

**Also confirm, as INPUTS require:** that no intermediary registration currently exists, that none
is planned, and whether a prior written opinion on this question exists anywhere in the company's
records.

**What we need back:** a written opinion on (a) and (b); any constraints on marketing copy that
follow from it (words or claims to avoid); and, if the answer is adverse for (b), which specific
activity crosses the line — the embedded offering can be narrowed activity-by-activity.

## Ask 2 — to the DPO · the Article 9 consent gate

**The request, precisely:**

> Please provide **(1)** the current status of external review of the GDPR Article 9 consent gate
> for AI processing of policy documents (completed / in progress / not started, and by whom), and
> **(2)** the reviewed, canonical consent wording — Greek and English — that the product is
> required to present.

**Why marketing needs the canonical text and will not paraphrase it:** the repositioning puts
consent language on the homepage trust bullets, on /trust, and on a new institutional partner page.
Marketing copy will **quote the reviewed wording**, never restate it — a reader who meets two
phrasings of one consent has met two consents. Until the canonical text arrives, those surfaces
carry placeholders and the partner page cannot ship.

**State of the risk profile — this is the part that changed, and you should know it plainly:**
the repositioning makes **private health insurance the lead proof of the product**. The front door
(hero rotation 1), the deepest consumer page (`/product/health`), and the planned guide cluster
all now route users toward uploading **health policies** — special-category data — as their *first*
action. The Article 9 gate has therefore moved from a pending sign-off on one processing path to
**the thing the front door depends on**. Volume and salience of Art. 9 processing both rise by
design. If the external review is not complete, that fact now sits on the critical path of a
marketing launch, and the launch holds for it.

**Also included for your review queue** *(flag-only items from the change list)*:
1. Terms §3 — the transfer pledge gains «χωρίς τη ρητή, ανακλητή σας συγκατάθεση» (decision D2);
   the /trust pledge copy follows the Terms, never precedes them.
2. Marketing data-location wording aligns to the legal pages (storage EU / analysis possibly
   ex-EU under DPF/SCCs) — direction marketing→legal; the legal pages are believed accurate.
3. Article 50 posture: the in-product AI disclaimer now states fallibility
   («μπορεί να περιέχουν λάθη — ελέγξτε τα πεδία») — confirm placement satisfies the posture.
4. /privacy and /cookies: no changes proposed — please sign off on "no change needed" so absence
   is a decision, not an oversight.

**What we need back:** items (1) and (2); a yes/no on whether the health-first repositioning
requires any change to the gate itself (wording, granularity, or placement); and a date.
